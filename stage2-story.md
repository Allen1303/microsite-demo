# Stage 2 — The Story: "The First Cup That Tastes Like Something"

One continuous narrative arc = the sales pitch. The transformation being sold:
**anonymous, stale, roasted-who-knows-when coffee → a cup roasted this week by a person
you can name, where the notes on the bag actually show up in your mouth.**

Working microsite concept: a single local artisan roastery brand (placeholder name:
**"First Crack Roasting Co."** — rename to the real client later). One product truth:
every bag stamped with a roast date from this week, roasted in small batches, picked up
or delivered locally within days of roast.

## The arc, shot by shot (continuous camera move — each shot begins where the last ended)

Each shot maps to a Stage 1 pain → relief beat. This outline IS the Stage 3 shot list
and the site's scroll structure.

| # | Shot (one continuous move) | Customer beat (from research) | On-screen copy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Slow push down a fluorescent grocery aisle. A wall of identical brown bags. No dates. No names. | "It just tastes like… coffee." The before-state. | "Most coffee was roasted months ago. Some of it, a year." |
| 2 | Camera pushes INTO one bag — darkness, dull matte grey-brown beans, lifeless, faint dust. | "Nothing had roast dates." Staleness made visible. | "By the time it reaches the shelf, the flavor is already gone." |
| 3 | A single bean ignites the transition: darkness warms to firelight — we emerge inside a spinning drum roaster, green beans tumbling, turning gold → amber. | The turn: a real person, a real fire, small batches. | "Ours never sits. It's roasted here, this week." |
| 4 | Beans crack (first crack) in macro slow-mo; oils glisten; camera pulls back to reveal a hand-stamped roast date: THIS WEEK'S DATE. | Roast date = the trust signal the research screams about. | "The roast date isn't fine print. It's the whole point." |
| 5 | The stamped bag tips; beans pour into a grinder → bloom shot: hot water hits fresh grounds, they swell and breathe, steam curls. | Anticipation. Freshness you can SEE (only fresh coffee blooms). | "Fresh coffee blooms. Stale coffee just sits there." |
| 6 | Camera rises with the steam and dives into the cup: deep amber, light refracting; a burst of abstract color — blueberry, caramel, citrus — blooming through the coffee like ink. | The conversion moment. "The blueberry shone through." | "The notes on the bag, finally in your cup. You're not a snob. You just hadn't had fresh coffee yet." |
| 7 | Pull back: the cup on a real kitchen counter, morning light, the stamped bag beside it. Stillness. Home. | Relief/resolution — this is YOUR Tuesday morning now. | CTA: "Get this week's roast." |

## Funnel & CTA

- **One primary CTA: "Get this week's roast"** — a real form: name, email, pickup or
  local delivery, whole bean or ground. Sits at shot 7's resting point and repeats in a
  persistent (mobile-thumb-reachable) bar.
- Secondary (de-emphasized, text link only): "Visit the roastery" (address/hours).
- The price objection ("is it worth it?") is answered inline before the form, in the
  research's own framing: a $18 bag ÷ ~25 cups ≈ 72¢ a cup — versus drinking stale coffee
  26 days a month. Honest math, no invented stats.

## Signature interaction (foreshadowing Stage 4)

**"The Bloom."** Press-and-hold the CTA-adjacent cup: holding "pours" — grounds bloom and
color-notes spread while you hold (mirrors shot 5–6). Touch-native, no hover needed —
derived from the research's single most-repeated joy moment (the bloom/first-taste).

## Voice guardrails for all copy
- Approachable craft. Confident, sensory, plain-spoken.
- Never: "curated," "elevated," "artisanal journey," cupping-score jargon.
- Real customer phrasing over marketing-speak (see Stage 1 copy bank).
- No fabricated testimonials/stats — the aisle/freshness claims stay within what the
  research supports (months-old shelf coffee; up to a year for some ground coffee).
