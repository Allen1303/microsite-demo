# Stage 1 — Customer Research: Local Artisan Coffee Roasters

Sources: real threads read directly on r/Coffee (old.reddit.com), cross-referenced with
web articles on roaster-vs-grocery freshness. Quotes below are verbatim or near-verbatim
from real posts; paraphrases are marked.

## Who the customer is

Not the coffee snob — the person *afraid of becoming* one. Grocery-coffee drinkers who
suspect there's something better, and recent converts evangelizing their first good cup.
The specialty-curious majority explicitly says the snob attitude repels them.

## Pain / desire / hesitation — in their own words

### 1. The conversion moment (the product's real promise)
- "I swore I was not a coffee snob... I've been enjoying grocery store coffee for years."
- "The blueberry shone through. The natural sweetness. ... To say it was the best cup of
  coffee I've ever had is an understatement." (bought from a local roaster's stall at an event)
- "I had no clue coffee had those notes. The bag smelled exactly like a strawberry."
- "Wow I never realized how big of a difference quality of beans makes!"
- "It's a whole other beverage."
- "It's worth the extra dollars to support local businesses and get a better quality product!"

### 2. Staleness is the enemy (and the grocery aisle is where coffee goes to die)
- Grocery coffee "may have been roasted up to a year before purchase"; national-brand
  distribution takes 2–3 months to shelf. Fresh coffee is best within ~3–14 days of roast.
- "Nothing had roast dates... the beans smelled like ashes and were unevenly roasted."
- "The same beans I bought yesterday smelled very acidic and tasted very burnt... their
  roasting level and date is very unreliable."
- Even mail subscriptions get accused of "passing off old beans as freshly roasted" —
  no roast date on the bag = suspicion. **Roast date is the trust signal.**
- "We made it the next day. And it WAS NOT THE SAME." (freshness decay is felt within days)

### 3. Tasting-note imposter syndrome (huge, underserved)
- "It's supposed to taste chocolate and butterscotch but I can't taste those... Is something
  wrong with my method or my tongue?"
- "This coffee is supposed to taste like shortbread, golden raisins, and blackberries and it
  just tastes like.. coffee."
- "I always thought about coffee notes as something only snobs could detect."
- "I will be damned if I can actually taste the notes the bean is meant to have."
- "I want to taste what you guys are tasting."
- And the joy when it lands: "For the first time since finding a local roaster I was able to
  taste the notes that were labeled on the package." / "I can finally taste the notes and
  fruity sweetness that I've been reading about!"

### 4. Price hesitation — "is it worth it?"
- "How expensive is too expensive for locally roasted single origin" — "$20 for a 340g bag...
  burning a hole in our pocket."
- "It's expensive as hell, is it worth it?"
- "Spending $15 on 12oz of coffee is proving a bit too expensive for me."
- "We have a few local roasters, but it's too expensive to make them our primary supply."
- The counter-experience that closes the sale: the grocery bag "actually tasted a bit stale"
  later in the week — cheap coffee's hidden cost is drinking stale coffee most of the month.
- "Many seem to be selling just average roasted beans at $6–8/100g... unlikely repeat
  customer... what am I missing here?" (price must be *earned* by the cup)

### 5. Snobbery / intimidation as purchase blocker
- "The term 'specialty coffee' sounds super pretentious and is probably a turn off to a lot
  of people who want to drink good coffee but hate that attitude."
- "Please don't judge me for drinking instant for so long."
- "I don't have the mental space or time to devote to getting really serious about coffee."
- Bad local shops make it worse: "The employee had no idea what the roast date on any of the
  coffee was." / "The owner is an asshole." (paraphrase kept: rude/gatekeepy staff kill it)

### 6. Subscription distrust + logistics friction
- "A lot of people complain about getting worse quality coffee as their service goes on."
- "I don't want to get behind or ahead and have too much/not enough coffee."
- Stale-beans complaints about big mail services → **local pickup / local delivery of
  this-week's roast is the differentiator big brands can't match.**

## Brand-voice calibration (governs all later stages)

**Approachable craft.** Serious about the roasting, deliberately unpretentious about the
drinker. Warm, sensory, plain-spoken; zero gatekeeping, zero jargon without translation.
- NOT luxury/exclusive (research shows exclusivity repels this audience)
- NOT playful/consumer-cute (undercuts the $18-a-bag price justification)
- The voice says: "You're not a snob. You just haven't had fresh coffee yet."

## Copy bank (real phrases to use, verbatim or close-paraphrase)
- "It just tastes like… coffee." (the before-state)
- "The blueberry shone through."
- "Roasted this week. Not last year."
- "You could actually taste a significant drop from day one to day two." → freshness urgency
- "Is it worth it?" → answer head-on, don't dodge
- "Nothing had roast dates." → our roast date is printed huge, on every bag
- "I want to taste what you guys are tasting." → promise: you will, on the first cup
